01Gravitational Fine-Tuning ReversionTraining-history geometry of post-alignment driftarXiv 2026Model AdaptationMechanistic UnderstandingAlignment RobustnessResearch questionCan fine-tuning reversion be explained through training-history geometry and causally reduced without sacrificing task utility?Open case studyClose+

Problem

Harmless fine-tuning can partially undo alignment, revive unlearned capabilities, or restore latent behaviours acquired earlier in training.

Safety risk

Benign post-alignment updates may reintroduce harmful behaviour while preserving downstream task performance, making safety erosion difficult to detect.

Methodology

  1. Model later alignment or specialization as a displacement from dominant behavioural manifolds formed earlier in training.
  2. Track whether representational drift acquires a component along a history-defined reversion direction, v_rev.
  3. Intervene by selectively blocking motion along v_rev during subsequent fine-tuning.
  4. Compare final alignment, harmfulness, and downstream task performance after the intervention.

Evaluation design

  • Post-alignment fine-tuning
  • Representation-space trajectory analysis
  • Directional causal intervention
  • Harmfulness and task-utility comparison

My contribution

First author on the work connecting fine-tuning reversion to training-history geometry and evaluating v_rev as a causal mediator of post-alignment drift.

Main findings

Representational drift rapidly aligns with v_rev; selectively blocking that direction changes final alignment and reduces harmfulness with little task cost in the evaluated setup.

Practical relevance

Provides a mechanistic signal and intervention target for maintaining safety when aligned models are customized after deployment.

Leadership / collaboration

Co-authored the study in a focused two-researcher collaboration linking mechanistic analysis to deployment safety decisions.

02SPQRSafety alignment under benign model adaptationECCV 2026Model AdaptationText-to-ImageBenchmarkingResearch questionHow should safety alignment be evaluated when text-to-image systems undergo benign deployment-time adaptation?Open case studyClose+

Problem

Safety controls in text-to-image systems can weaken after ordinary fine-tuning, even when the downstream task and data are benign.

Safety risk

A model may pass its original safety evaluation but become less reliable after customization, while still appearing useful on standard quality measures.

Methodology

  1. Build a multidimensional benchmark around safety alignment after fine-tuning.
  2. Compare benign adaptation settings under consistent evaluation conditions.
  3. Measure safety alongside prompt adherence, image quality, and robustness.
  4. Analyse trade-offs that a single aggregate score would conceal.

Evaluation design

  • Safety retention
  • Prompt adherence
  • Image quality
  • Adaptation robustness
  • Cross-method comparison

My contribution

Co-authored the work and advised project development on benchmark design, experimentation, and publication for harmful-content mitigation in text-to-image models.

Main findings

The benchmark shows why safety alignment must be re-evaluated after benign adaptation rather than treated as a fixed pre-deployment property.

Practical relevance

Gives safety teams a deployment-aware framework for deciding whether a customized generative model remains safe enough to release.

Leadership / collaboration

Provided research mentorship on design, implementation, experimental review, and publication strategy.

03Safe-CLIPRemoving NSFW concepts from vision-language modelsECCV 2024Vision-LanguageMachine UnlearningGuardrailsResearch questionCan unsafe concepts be removed from CLIP representations while preserving useful vision-language capabilities?Open case studyClose+

Problem

Vision-language representations can preserve unsafe concept associations that propagate into downstream retrieval and generation systems.

Safety risk

Representation-level associations can make harmful visual-textual content difficult to filter consistently without damaging useful model behaviour.

Methodology

  1. Target unsafe concept associations directly in the vision-language representation space.
  2. Use safety-focused data to guide concept removal.
  3. Evaluate retained utility alongside harmful-content behaviour.
  4. Test the method across downstream vision-language tasks.

Evaluation design

  • NSFW concept removal
  • Benign capability retention
  • Vision-language transfer
  • Safety-aware representation analysis

My contribution

First author; led the research on safety-aware representation learning, experimental evaluation, and technical communication.

Main findings

Safe-CLIP reduces unsafe concept associations in CLIP while preserving useful vision-language behaviour across evaluated tasks.

Practical relevance

Connects representation-level intervention to practical guardrails, content-safety evaluation, and multimodal model assurance.

Leadership / collaboration

Coordinated the research with collaborators at AImageLab and contributed to the open-source code, dataset, and project release.

04Multilingual AI Safety at Meta GenAIIndustry research experienceMeta GenAI Trust & Safety - May to Nov 2024Multilingual SafetyIndustry ResearchRed TeamingResearch questionHow robustly does safety behaviour transfer across languages, and how does model adaptation change that robustness?Open case studyClose+

Problem

Safety behaviour that is reliable in one language may not transfer consistently across languages or survive later fine-tuning.

Safety risk

Uneven safeguards create cross-lingual attack surfaces in globally deployed generative AI systems.

Methodology

  1. Evaluate Llama safety across multiple language conditions.
  2. Apply cross-lingual red-teaming and fine-tuning attacks.
  3. Compare safety behaviour before and after adaptation.
  4. Interpret results for globally deployed generative AI systems.

Evaluation design

  • Cross-language comparisons
  • Adversarial fine-tuning
  • Safeguard-transfer analysis
  • Deployment-oriented interpretation

My contribution

Conducted research on multilingual LLM safety, red teaming, and Llama models as a Research Scientist Intern within Meta GenAI Trust & Safety.

Main findings

The publicly disclosed research shows that fine-tuning attacks in one language can degrade multilingual safety alignment.

Practical relevance

Addresses a core assurance challenge for AI systems expected to operate safely across global user populations.

Leadership / collaboration

Collaborated with an industrial research team while translating an open safety question into reproducible cross-lingual evaluation.

06AI Security, Watermarking & ForensicsAdversarial research portfolioPublished and preprint work - 2024 to 2026AI SecurityWatermarkingDeepfakesResearch questionWhich security and authenticity claims remain valid under fine-tuning, forgery attempts, and adaptive attacks?Open case studyClose+

Problem

Security mechanisms can fail under model modification, adaptive attacks, or threat models that differ from the original evaluation.

Safety risk

Watermarks, monitors, and authenticity signals may be bypassed or forged if they are evaluated only under average-case conditions.

Methodology

  1. Define the asset, adversary, and security boundary.
  2. Test mechanisms under plausible post-training model changes.
  3. Evaluate watermark forgery and activation-based monitoring.
  4. Connect model-level evidence with multimedia authenticity analysis.

Evaluation design

  • Activation watermarking
  • Watermark forgery resistance
  • Fine-tuning attacks
  • Deepfake robustness
  • Adaptive evaluation

My contribution

Co-authored work on activation watermarking and randomized-key defenses, and mentored researchers working on watermarking and deepfake analysis.

Main findings

The portfolio treats robustness under adaptation and adaptive attack as part of the security claim, not as a secondary check.

Practical relevance

Brings an explicit adversary and evidence model to safety monitoring, provenance, and authenticity decisions.

Leadership / collaboration

Supported research planning, project execution, experimental review, and publication strategy across several collaborative workstreams.

    Map failure modes
